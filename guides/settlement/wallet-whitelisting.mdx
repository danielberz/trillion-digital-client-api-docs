---
title: "Wallet whitelisting"
description: "Registering and screening crypto destinations."
---

Crypto withdrawals go only to whitelisted addresses. Whitelisting registers an
address, screens it, and binds it to a specific asset and network.

## Why it exists

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Irreversibility" icon="triangle-exclamation">
    On-chain transfers cannot be recalled. Pre-approving destinations is the only
    effective control against a mistyped or substituted address.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Screening obligations" icon="shield-halved">
    Destinations are screened against sanctions lists and analytics on illicit
    activity before funds can move.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Registering an address

<Steps>
  <Step title="Submit the address">
    Provide the address, the **asset**, and the **network**, plus a memo or
    destination tag if the network requires one. You may be sent a secure link to
    submit directly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Screening">
    The address is screened for sanctions exposure and links to illicit activity.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verification">
    Depending on the address type, ownership verification may be required.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approval">
    Once approved the address becomes available as a withdrawal destination.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
Whitelisting is not instant. Screening and verification take time, and an address
that fails screening will not be approved at all. Register destinations well
before you need them.
</Warning>

## Naming your addresses

Each whitelisted address carries a label.

<Warning>
Labels must be **unique** within your account. Submitting a new address under a
label already in use is the usual cause of an apparent "only one wallet was
accepted" outcome — the second submission collides with the first on name rather
than on address. Use distinct, descriptive labels such as
`Treasury-USDC-Ethereum` rather than `Wallet 1`.
</Warning>

## Asset and network binding

An approved address is approved for **one asset on one network**. The same
address string used for a different asset or a different chain requires a
separate registration.

<Warning>
This is not bureaucracy. The same address can be syntactically valid on several
EVM networks while funds are only recoverable on one. Binding the approval to a
specific network is what prevents an unrecoverable transfer.
</Warning>

## Ongoing screening

Whitelisted addresses are re-screened over time. An address that later becomes
associated with sanctioned or illicit activity can be suspended, and pending
withdrawals to it stopped.

## Removing an address

Request removal through the platform or your account manager as soon as an
address is no longer in use. Keeping the whitelist tight is a meaningful control,
not housekeeping.

## Over the API

Reference an approved address by `AddressID` on
[`NewWithdrawRequest`](/trading-api/websocket/commands#newwithdrawrequest).
`AddressID` and inline `RoutingInfo` are mutually exclusive — prefer `AddressID`,
since routing information that does not map to an approved address will not
short-circuit the approval process.
