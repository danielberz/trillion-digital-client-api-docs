## Access and credentials

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How do I get API credentials?">
    Email [support@trilliondigital.io](mailto:support@trilliondigital.io) stating
    which environment you need. Sandbox and production keys are issued
    separately and are not interchangeable.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I use my sandbox key against production?">
    No. Keys are environment-specific. The host is also part of the signed
    payload, so pointing a sandbox integration at production requires changing
    both the URL and the string you sign.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I was sent to the live terminal but I want sandbox.">
    Sandbox is at `sandbox.trading.trilliondigital.io`. If a link took you
    somewhere else, use that address directly and tell your account manager.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Who can act on my account from a chat channel?">
    Anyone who can post in it, to the extent of their role. Three levels are set
    per member when the channel is provisioned: **view** sees prices,
    instruments, and recent activity; **quote** can open an RFQ but cannot commit
    the account; **book** can book, amend, and cancel. Send `/help` in your own
    channel — it reports what *your* role can do, which no documentation table
    can. See
    [Chat permissions](/guides/trading/execution-channels#permissions).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Someone with access has left. What do we need to tell you?">
    Say specifically **which API keys to revoke**, not just which login to
    disable. API keys are not tied to a browser session, so revoking someone's
    portal access does not stop an integration running under a key they held.
    Remove them from the dealing channel at the same time. See
    [Removing access](/guides/accounts/users-and-roles#removing-access).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Authentication

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Every request returns 401.">
    In order of likelihood: the timestamp does not have exactly six fractional
    digits; the host in the signed string includes `https://` or a port; the
    signature is standard rather than URL-safe base64; or the same timestamp was
    not used in both the header and the signed payload. See
    [Authentication](/trading-api/authentication).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="It worked, then started failing.">
    Almost always clock drift. Signatures are timestamp-bound — sync against NTP.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I cache the signature?">
    No. It is valid only for the moment it was generated. This is why a saved
    Postman request fails on its second send — see
    [Postman](/trading-api/sdks/postman).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Trading

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="My order was rejected. Why?">
    Check `OrdRejReason` on the `ExecutionReport`. The most common causes are
    increment violations and `OrderExceedsLimit`. See
    [Errors](/trading-api/errors).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I sent an order but saw no result.">
    Subscribe to `Order` and `ExecutionReport` **before** sending commands.
    Command responses are acknowledgements — the outcome is published on the
    stream, so subscribing afterwards means missing it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does OrderQty mean coins or currency?">
    Whichever `Currency` says. On `BTC-USD` with `Currency: "BTC"` it is
    bitcoin; with `Currency: "USD"` it is dollars of notional. Always set it
    explicitly.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="My quote expired before I could accept it.">
    Quotes expire — by default after about three minutes, with `ValidUntilTime`
    authoritative. Request a new one; the price may differ.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I have a balance but orders are rejected for funds.">
    You are likely sizing against `Amount` rather than `AvailableAmount`.
    Available excludes funds committed to working orders and pending
    withdrawals.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Pricing

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="AvgPx and my own P&L disagree.">
    `AvgPx` on an [`ExecutionReport`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/order) is
    the average fill price **excluding fees**. `TradedPx` on a
    [`Quote`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/quote) is all-in and **includes**
    them. A book marked off `AvgPx` understates cost by exactly the fee, every
    time and in the same direction — so it reconciles cleanly against itself.

    Reconcile from the [`Trade`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/trade) stream,
    which carries `Fee`, `FeeCurency`, and `Amount` explicitly. Do not assume the
    fee currency: on a partially filled order the `ExecutionReport`'s
    `LastFeeCurency` can differ from the cumulative `FeeCurency`.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does the channel I trade through change my price?">
    No. The same trade in the same size prices the same by phone, chat, portal,
    or API — see
    [They converge](/guides/trading/execution-channels#they-converge).

    What does change the price is **size relative to the market**, which is a
    different question from how the request reached us. A fully automated desk
    should still call for a block that would move the screen.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The streaming price and the RFQ price differ.">
    A
    [`MarketDataSnapshot`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/market-data-snapshot)
    publishes levels against the security's `SizeBuckets`; an RFQ returns a firm
    price for the size you actually asked for. Comparing a top-of-book level
    against a quote in size is not like-for-like. If a screen price looks wrong
    for the size you want, call the desk rather than trade on it — see
    [How OTC trading works](/guides/trading/how-otc-trading-works).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I mark my book off the CurrencyConversion rate?">
    Not for anything that matters. The stream is deliberately lazy: updates are
    throttled to at most one every `Throttle` — minimum **and** default `10s` —
    and are suppressed entirely until the rate moves by at least `Tolerance`,
    minimum and default `0.0001`. A rate can therefore be ten seconds old and a
    basis point off while the stream is behaving exactly as specified.

    Use it for display and reporting; price and size from market data or an RFQ.
    For balances you do not need it at all — the
    [`Balance`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/balance) subscription takes an
    `EquivalentCurrency` parameter and returns converted amounts inline. See
    [CurrencyConversion](/trading-api/websocket/streams/currency#currencyconversion).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Assets and networks

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How do I find what I can actually trade?">
    Your account's live list, and nothing else. Over the WebSocket API that is
    the [`Security`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/security) stream; in the
    terminal it is the instrument selector; in chat it is `/pairs`. Over FIX,
    request the security list — `SecurityListRequestType` supports only `4`
    (ALL_SECURITIES), so there is no server-side symbol filter and you filter
    client-side.

    Securities are added and withdrawn over time. On the `Security` stream a
    withdrawal arrives as `UpdateAction: "Remove"`, and a client that ignores it
    carries on quoting and sizing against something it can no longer trade. See
    [Instruments](/guides/trading/instruments).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The same asset exists on two chains. Does that matter?">
    More than the address does. A whitelisted destination is approved for **one
    asset on one network**, and the same address string can be syntactically
    valid on several EVM chains while the funds are only recoverable on one of
    them. Sending on the wrong network is generally unrecoverable — there is no
    support process that reverses it, because there is nothing to reverse.

    Register the same address separately for each asset and network you intend to
    use it for. See
    [Asset and network binding](/guides/settlement/wallet-whitelisting#asset-and-network-binding).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Which networks and assets does the platform support?">
    [Assets and networks](/guides/trading/assets-and-networks) lists the networks
    and assets the platform models.

    <Warning>
    That page is **what the platform supports, not what is enabled on your
    account.** What you can trade follows from your entitlements, your facing
    [entity](/guides/accounts/entities), and your commercial terms; what you can
    withdraw follows from your whitelist. Your onboarding documentation is the
    actual set, and your account manager is authoritative where the two differ.
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Which chains need a memo or destination tag?">
    The ones where a single address serves many holders and the tag is what
    distinguishes them. Of the networks the platform models, those are Ripple,
    Stellar, Cosmos, TON, and Algorand — see
    [Memo and destination-tag networks](/guides/trading/assets-and-networks#memo-and-destination-tag-networks).
    The registration form asks for the tag wherever the network requires one.

    <Warning>
    Omitting a required memo does not fail. The transfer confirms on-chain and
    the explorer shows a success, while the receiving institution has funds it
    cannot attribute to you — recovery is manual at the far end and sometimes
    does not happen at all. Register the memo alongside the address so it is
    applied automatically rather than remembered. See
    [Memos and destination tags](/guides/settlement/crypto#memos-and-destination-tags).
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Connectivity

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="My WebSocket goes quiet but never disconnects.">
    A half-open TCP connection. It never fires a close event, so reconnect logic
    never triggers. Send periodic pings and force a reconnect when traffic stops
    — see [Connection lifecycle](/trading-api/connection-lifecycle).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="After reconnecting I get no data.">
    Subscriptions do not survive a disconnect. Replay them on reconnect.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="My history query is missing older records.">
    The stream paged and the `next` value was not followed. See
    [Subscriptions](/trading-api/websocket/subscriptions#page-a-large-stream).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Reporting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="My end-of-day reconciliation is short of the blotter.">
    Check paging before investigating anything else. `Trade`, `Order`, and
    `BalanceTransaction` page over a wide date range: the payload carries a
    `next` value, and a client that does not echo it back in a `page` request
    receives the first page only. No error is raised, so the job reports success
    on a truncated set — see
    [Page a large stream](/trading-api/websocket/subscriptions#page-a-large-stream).

    Then subscribe with `StartDate` set to the `Timestamp` of the last trade you
    durably recorded rather than to midnight. Overlap costs nothing, because
    `TradeID` makes reprocessing idempotent.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A trade I already recorded has disappeared.">
    It has not been deleted — its `TradeStatus` moved. A trade can go to
    `Canceled` **after** appearing as `Pending` or `Confirmed`.

    The mirror image is the dangerous one: a booking system that treats the first
    `Trade` message it sees as final never learns about the cancellation and
    carries the trade indefinitely. Key on `TradeID` and always apply the latest
    status. See
    [Trade lifecycle](/guides/trading/trade-lifecycle).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Every buy and sell looks inverted against my system.">
    `Side` is stated from **your** perspective, on the confirmation and on the
    [`Trade`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/trade) stream. A downstream system
    that records the dealer's side shows every trade backwards — and because it
    is wrong uniformly, the totals still foot and the feed looks healthy. See
    [Reading a confirmation](/guides/trading/trade-lifecycle#reading-a-confirmation).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Settlement

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I withdraw to an address not on the whitelist?">
    No. Every destination must be registered and screened first. See
    [Wallet whitelisting](/guides/settlement/wallet-whitelisting).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I submitted several wallets and only one was accepted.">
    Usually a label collision — labels must be unique within your account, so a
    second submission reusing a label conflicts with the first. Resubmit with
    distinct labels.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="My wire was returned.">
    Most often an incomplete beneficiary address. Wire rails require street,
    town, and country on both the beneficiary and the bank. See
    [Fiat settlement](/guides/settlement/fiat).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="My wire was refused because the beneficiary banks with the same institution.">
    Some banks will not wire to their own customer and require their internal
    rail instead. The beneficiary has to be re-registered with that network's
    identifier — a rail address, UUID, or email depending on the network. See
    [Internal settlement networks](/guides/settlement/fiat#internal-settlement-networks).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I use an internal rail to move money between my own accounts?">
    No. Internal networks pay *other* customers of the same institution; a move
    between your own accounts is a book transfer. Institutions enforce this and
    will refuse a self-addressed rail transfer outright.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why can't I edit a registered bank account or rail address?">
    Changes are treated as a new registration so they are re-verified and
    re-screened. Rail addresses in particular are resolved at creation and are
    immutable afterwards. See
    [Bank account whitelisting](/guides/settlement/bank-accounts).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I cancel a withdrawal?">
    Only while it is `PendingApproval`. Once it reaches `PendingTransfer` it
    cannot be recalled — and once an on-chain transaction is broadcast, nobody
    can recall it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can you pay a third party?">
    Settlement is to accounts in your own name. Third-party payments need
    compliance review in advance.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Banking and entities

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="A currency or rail I have read about is not available to me.">
    The rail and currency tables on
    [Fiat settlement](/guides/settlement/fiat) are what the **platform**
    supports. What you can use follows from the Trillion Digital entity you face
    and the institutions that entity banks with, and that is settled at
    onboarding rather than at instruction time. A currency or internal network
    offered through one entity may simply not exist on another.

    Ask before concluding it is unavailable: some internal networks are arranged
    through your account manager and do not appear in the self-service
    registration form even when they are open to you. See
    [Entities](/guides/accounts/entities).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A destination I registered is not visible on my other account.">
    Entities are operationally segregated. Each is a separate account with
    separate API credentials, separate settlement instructions, and a separate
    whitelist — registering a bank account or wallet against one does not
    register it against the other, even though it is the same underlying account
    in the same name. Balances do not net across them and credit extended to one
    is not headroom for the other.

    <Warning>
    This fails quietly rather than loudly. The symptom is a validation error for
    a destination that obviously exists, because it exists on the *other*
    account — so the instinct is to re-register it, which produces a second
    registration and a second thing to keep current. Confirm which account you
    are operating on first. See
    [Working with more than one entity](/guides/accounts/entities#working-with-more-than-one-entity).
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Which banking partners sit behind my account?">
    Dedicated integrations exist with Anchorage, Cobre, Cross River, Customers
    Bank, Erebor, and FV Bank. Which of them sits behind *your* account follows
    from your entity, and it is why one client can settle over an internal
    network another has never been offered.

    <Warning>
    That is the platform's set of integrations, not a list of banks available to
    you. Your onboarding documentation names the partners that apply to your
    account; your account manager is authoritative.
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Operating

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="I traded on Friday and it settled Monday.">
    Each rail has a daily cut-off, and an instruction after it is processed on
    the next business day — which on a Friday is Monday, and later where a
    holiday falls on either side. Cut-offs vary by rail, currency, and
    jurisdiction, so a Friday afternoon comfortably inside the cut-off for one
    rail is outside it for another.

    The cut-offs and holiday calendars applicable to your account are in your
    onboarding documentation — confirm them with your account manager rather than
    inferring them from a payment that happened to arrive quickly. Book transfers
    and internal networks sidestep the problem entirely, because they never reach
    an external clearing system. See
    [Cut-off times](/guides/settlement/overview#cut-off-times).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="One leg of my trade has settled and the other has not.">
    Balances update as **each leg completes**, and the two legs move over
    unrelated infrastructure. A crypto leg credits once it reaches the
    confirmation threshold for that asset and network; a fiat leg follows its
    rail and the banking calendar. A crypto leg crediting well ahead of the fiat
    leg is the ordinary shape of a cross-asset trade, not a fault.

    Raise anything that looks wrong **before** the value date. See
    [Trade lifecycle](/guides/trading/trade-lifecycle).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A trade I did over the phone is not in the blotter yet.">
    Booking a voice trade is a human step — it appears once the trader has booked
    it, whereas portal and API orders book immediately. Nothing downstream
    differs: same blotter, same `TradeID`, same settlement path. If it has not
    appeared, raise it rather than waiting it out. See
    [One ledger](/guides/trading/execution-channels#one-ledger-updated-in-real-time).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
