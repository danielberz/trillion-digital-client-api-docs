Reconciling against Trillion Digital is a two-way comparison, not a consolidation
exercise. There is one record of your account and every execution channel writes
to it, so a break is a difference between your book and a single platform record
— see
[One ledger](/guides/trading/execution-channels#one-ledger-updated-in-real-time).
A voice trade and an API order carry the same `TradeID` and sit in the same
blotter.

Most breaks turn out to be defects in how the data was extracted rather than
disagreements about the trade, and it is the same handful of defects every time.

## Where the data comes from

| Source | Carries | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Portal blotter** | Trades, balances, and settlement status on screen | Settling "what actually happened" without writing code |
| **[`Trade`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/trade) stream with `StartDate`** | Post-trade records, machine-readable, with fees and amounts stated | Automated reconciliation and backfill |
| **[`Balance` and `BalanceTransaction`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/balance)** | Current balances, and the deposits, withdrawals and adjustments that moved them | Cash and position reconciliation |
| **[`Exposure`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/credit)** | Credit utilisation against your limit | Reconciling headroom, if you trade on credit |
| **Statements and confirmations** | The documents, issued per entity | Books and records, audit, and disputes |

Reconcile against `Trade`, not against the order streams. `Order` and
`ExecutionReport` describe how an order behaved; only the `Trade` record states
`Fee` and `Amount` explicitly, so a P&L assembled from execution reports is
inferring figures that the trade record already gives you.

<Note>
`Timestamp` and `TransactTime` are not the same field and should not feed the
same column. `TransactTime` is when the trade was executed — the trade date for
your books. `Timestamp` is when the message was published, which for a
high-touch trade is when the trader booked it, and that is a human step. Use
`Timestamp` for your ingest watermark and `TransactTime` for the trade date.
</Note>

## Pulling history

The Trading API is WebSocket-first. Historical reads are subscriptions carrying a
`StartDate`, not endpoint polls — REST serves historical OHLCV bars and nothing
else, as set out in [Endpoints](/trading-api/rest/endpoints).

Three rules make a repeatable ingest safe:

- **Watermark on the last record you durably stored**, not on the clock time your
  job ran. Records booked between your last read and your clock reading are
  otherwise skipped and never looked for again.
- **Overlap deliberately.** Re-subscribing from slightly before the watermark
  costs nothing, because `TradeID` makes the ingest idempotent — provided you
  upsert rather than append.
- **Follow `next` to exhaustion** before you treat a backfill as complete. See
  [Page a large stream](/trading-api/websocket/subscriptions#page-a-large-stream).

## What breaks a reconciliation

### A status that changed after you recorded it

<Warning>
`TradeStatus` can move to `Canceled` after a trade has already appeared as
`Pending` or `Confirmed`. A system that records the first message it sees as
final carries that trade on its books indefinitely — a position that will never
settle, hedged against something that no longer exists, and it will not surface
as a break because your book is internally consistent.

Key on `TradeID` and apply the latest status. Treat the feed as a stream of
upserts, not as an append-only journal. See
[Trade status](/guides/trading/trade-lifecycle#trade-status).
</Warning>

Upserting earns its keep twice over: an append-only ingest also double-counts
every record in the overlap window the moment you re-subscribe after a
disconnect.

### The first page that looks like the whole history

<Warning>
Wide historical subscriptions **page**. A client that ignores the `next` value on
the payload receives the first page, no error, and no marker where the data
stops. The job completes, the log is clean, and the extract is short.

Nothing downstream can detect a record that was never delivered — a match rate
computed over the extract comes out clean precisely because the missing trades
are absent from both sides of the comparison.
</Warning>

<Tip>
Assert that `next` is absent before you mark a backfill complete, rather than
eyeballing whether the row count looks plausible. Then test it: request a range
you know exceeds one page and check that the count matches the blotter for the
same window.
</Tip>

### Side is stated from your perspective

<Warning>
`Side` is `Buy` or `Sell` **from your side of the trade**. A downstream system
that records the dealer's side will look inverted on every row.

Quantities, prices and fees all agree, so a reconciliation that matches on
notional passes cleanly while every position in the book faces the wrong way.
The error is uniform rather than sporadic, which means it produces no breaks to
investigate — it usually surfaces as a P&L or exposure figure with the right
magnitude and the wrong sign, well after the feed went live.

Confirm which convention a downstream system expects before wiring anything into
it. See
[Reading a confirmation](/guides/trading/trade-lifecycle#reading-a-confirmation).
</Warning>

### Decimals parsed as floats

Quantities, prices, fees and amounts arrive as **string-encoded decimals**.
Parse them into a decimal type; never into an IEEE float, and never send them
back as JSON numbers.

<Warning>
A float round-trip produces differences a few units in the last place — too
small to be a real trade discrepancy, too large for a tolerance of zero. They
cannot be explained by anything on the platform side, because they were created
by your own decoder.

The same defect on the order-entry path is louder: an increment violation is
rejected outright. See
[Precision and floats](/guides/trading/instruments#precision-and-floats).
</Warning>

### The other entity's trades

Groups facing two Trillion Digital entities run two genuinely separate
relationships. API keys are issued per entity account, there is no cross-entity
view over the API, and statements and confirmations are issued per entity.

<Warning>
Comparing a group-level book against an extract pulled with one entity's key
returns every trade of the other entity as an unmatched break. The volume of
breaks makes it look like a platform failure, and it is a mapping error on your
side — aggregation across entities is your responsibility, by design. See
[Entities](/guides/accounts/entities).
</Warning>

## Reference data your mapping has to handle

The asset and network sets the platform models are listed on
[Assets and networks](/guides/trading/assets-and-networks); the fiat currencies,
rails and internal settlement networks — which carry their own rail codes rather
than presenting as a wire — are on [Fiat settlement](/guides/settlement/fiat).
Parse against those supersets rather than against the subset your account has
happened to trade so far.

<Warning>
**A ticker is not a key.** An asset can exist on several networks at once, and a
whitelisted address is registered for a specific asset on a specific network —
see [Crypto settlement](/guides/settlement/crypto). Key balances, transfers and
addresses on **asset plus network**.

Collapsing them onto the ticker merges holdings that are not interchangeable at
settlement time, and the consequence is not a reporting error. It is a
withdrawal instructed against an address your mapping resolved on the wrong
network, which is generally unrecoverable.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**Those lists are what the platform supports, not what you are offered.** A
currency, network or asset the platform models is not necessarily enabled on
your account, and the set differs between entities. Your onboarding
documentation and your account manager are authoritative on what you can
actually trade and settle.
</Warning>

## Reporting over REST

<Warning>
Request/response reporting over REST is what the
[Platform API](/platform-api/overview) is being designed to provide, and **it is
not available yet.** The contract is published for review — trade, balance and
transaction history, statements and exports, with cursor
[pagination](/platform-api/pagination) and idempotency keys — but nothing on
those pages can be called today. Until it ships, historical reads run through
WebSocket subscriptions with a `StartDate`.
</Warning>

The design is open for comment. If a field you need to match against your own
book is absent from the specification, say so now —
[support@trilliondigital.io](mailto:support@trilliondigital.io).

## Triage: symptom to cause

The portal blotter is the same record the API serves, not a separate view that
could have drifted. Checking there first locates the fault on one side or the
other.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trades on your book that never settle | Status moved to `Canceled` after you recorded it | Re-pull by `TradeID` and apply the latest `TradeStatus` |
| History shorter than expected, no errors | The `next` cursor was ignored | Assert `next` is absent at the end of the run |
| Every position facing the wrong way | `Side` convention mismatch | Compare one trade whose direction you know independently |
| Breaks of a few units in the last place | Decimals parsed as floats | Inspect your decoder, not the platform record |
| A whole account's trades missing | Extract pulled with the other entity's key | Confirm which entity account the key faces |
| A recent voice trade absent | Not yet booked by the trader | Check the portal blotter, then raise it with your trader |

Raise anything that survives that list with the `TradeID`, to your account
manager or
[support@trilliondigital.io](mailto:support@trilliondigital.io). Settlement
discrepancies go in **before the value date**.
