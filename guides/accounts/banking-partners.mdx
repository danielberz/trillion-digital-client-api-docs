title: "Banking partners"
description: "Which institutions hold client fiat, what that determines, and which questions only your onboarding documentation can answer."
---

Client-designated fiat is held with regulated banking partners. Which of them
apply to you follows from the Trillion Digital
[entity](/guides/accounts/entities) you face — settled at onboarding, not chosen
at instruction time. The rails you can instruct, the internal networks a
beneficiary is reachable on, and the currencies you can hold all sit downstream
of that relationship.

## Partners the platform integrates with

Dedicated integrations exist for **Anchorage, Cobre, Cross River, Customers Bank,
Erebor and FV Bank**. An integration means the platform instructs payments and
reads balances against that bank programmatically rather than through a file drop
or a manual process.

<Warning>
**A partner on this list is not a partner on your account.** This is what the
platform integrates with, not what holds your balances. Which institutions hold
yours depends on your entity, your currencies and your commercial terms, and your
onboarding documentation is the record of it.

The cost of assuming otherwise surfaces at registration: you register a
beneficiary expecting an internal rail, the platform registers it for a wire
instead, and every subsequent payment to that beneficiary carries
correspondent-banking cost, delay and the failure modes an on-us transfer never
touches. Ask your account manager which institutions apply before you build an
operational assumption on one.
</Warning>

<Note>
An integration is not the same thing as an internal settlement network. An
integration is *how the platform talks to a bank*; an internal network is an
*addressing mechanism* for a beneficiary who banks where we bank.
[Fiat settlement](/guides/settlement/fiat) documents internal networks at
institutions with no dedicated integration here, and neither list tells you what
is enabled on your account.
</Note>

## The partner determines the rails

Currency does not determine reachability on its own. USD can move by Fedwire,
ACH, FedNow, RTP or SWIFT, or across any of several internal networks — which of
those are open to you is a function of who we bank with for your entity and who
your beneficiary banks with.

[Fiat settlement](/guides/settlement/fiat) sets out the full rail and currency
set the platform supports, how a rail is chosen for a given payment, and what
each one needs from a registration. What is enabled on your account is a narrower
set; your account manager is authoritative on it.

## Your entity determines the banking relationship

Each Trillion Digital entity banks with its own institutions — see
[Entities](/guides/accounts/entities).

<Warning>
Groups facing two entities should expect **the same beneficiary to behave
differently on each**. A counterparty reachable on an internal network from one
account may be reachable only by wire from the other, because that entity does
not bank at the same institution. The addressing details are not the same
details, so copying an instruction between accounts produces a registration that
fails validation or, worse, one that is accepted and then routes expensively.
</Warning>

## Digital assets

Crypto settles on-chain to addresses that have been
[whitelisted](/guides/settlement/wallet-whitelisting), screened, and bound to one
asset on one network. There is no ad-hoc withdrawal path — see
[Crypto settlement](/guides/settlement/crypto) for how a transfer progresses and
where it stops being cancellable, and
[Assets and networks](/guides/trading/assets-and-networks) for the assets and
networks the platform models and where a wrong-chain transfer is unrecoverable.

Both of those are lists of what the platform supports. What is enabled for you is
narrower: the [`Security`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/security) stream and
your terminal's instrument selector are authoritative for trading — see
[Instruments](/guides/trading/instruments) — and your account manager for which
networks you can settle on.

## Segregation

Accounts, balances, settlement instructions and trade records belong to one
entity and do not commingle across entities. Balances are not fungible between
them, a destination registered against one is not visible to the other, and API
credentials are issued per entity account. [Entities](/guides/accounts/entities)
covers the operational consequences.

<Note>
That is a statement about how the platform is organised operationally. It is not
a statement about legal title, insolvency treatment, or how funds are held by any
banking partner — see below.
</Note>

## What this page does not tell you

Deliberately absent, because they are legal and regulatory statements rather than
platform behaviour:

- whether balances attract deposit insurance, and under which scheme
- the regulatory status, licences or permissions of Trillion Digital or any
  banking partner
- how funds would be treated in an insolvency of Trillion Digital, an entity, or
  a partner institution
- whether client-money or equivalent rules apply to your relationship
- which named institution holds your balance, in whose name it is held, and
  whether the account is segregated or pooled
- who custodies digital assets held on your account

Those are answered in the documentation executed at onboarding, which is specific
to your entity and your relationship. For diligence, route the request through
your account manager, or
[compliance@trilliondigital.io](mailto:compliance@trilliondigital.io) for
documentation already issued to you.

<Warning>
Do not cite this page — or any product documentation — as the source for an

counterparty's own reviewer asks for the underlying document. Request the
document.
</Warning>
