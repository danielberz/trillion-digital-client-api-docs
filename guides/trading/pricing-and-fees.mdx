Rates, spreads, and fee schedules are commercial terms agreed for your account at
onboarding. They are not documented here — your account manager and your terms of
business are authoritative on what you pay.

## A quote is all-in for the size you asked for

You ask for a price in a specific size and receive one price for that whole size.
There is no walk-up through a book, no partial fills at successively worse levels,
and no slippage caused by your own order. The number you accept is the number you
trade at, for the full clip, until `ValidUntilTime` — see
[How OTC trading works](/guides/trading/how-otc-trading-works).

The consequence is a comparison trap. A quote for size is not comparable to the
top-of-book price on a venue, because top-of-book is available only in
top-of-book size. The honest benchmark is the **all-in cost of working the same
quantity** on that venue — exchange fees, the levels you would actually clear
through, and the market impact of showing the order — against the single quoted
price here.

The price is made for the quantity in the request, so
[which currency you express that quantity in](/guides/trading/rfq-workflow#specifying-size)
changes what you are asking for.

## Which field carries which number

Four fields express price or cost, and they do not mean the same thing. Using the
wrong one does not produce an error — it produces a plausible number that is
wrong in a consistent direction.

| Field | Stream | Fees | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `TradedPx` | [`Quote`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/quote), on a filled RFQ | **Includes** fees | All-in economics off the quote itself |
| `AvgPx` | [`ExecutionReport`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/order) | **Excludes** fees | Execution quality, against other ex-fee prices |
| `Fee` + `FeeCurency` | [`Trade`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/trade) | The fee, stated explicitly | P&L, invoice checks, reconciliation |
| `LastFee` + `LastFeeCurency` | [`ExecutionReport`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/order) | Fee on that fill only | Attributing cost fill by fill |

<Warning>
**Reconciling P&L from `AvgPx` understates your cost, and nothing will tell you.**
The error is one-directional and scales with volume, so it does not net out over
time and it does not surface as a break — your numbers simply drift cheap against
the statement. Compute economics from the
[`Trade`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/trade) record, which carries `Amount`
and `Fee` explicitly.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**The field is spelled `FeeCurency` on the wire — one `r`.** So are
`LastFeeCurency` and the cumulative `FeeCurency` on the order stream. A
deserialiser looking for `FeeCurrency` finds nothing, and in a language that
returns `undefined` rather than raising, that becomes a fee with no currency —
which downstream code usually treats as the trade currency. Match the wire
spelling and alias it in your own model rather than correcting it in transit.
</Warning>

## Fee currency is its own field

A trade carries three currency fields: `Currency` for the quantity,
`AmountCurrency` for the amount, and `FeeCurency` for the fee. The third is not
constrained to match either of the other two. Which currency your fees are
charged in is a commercial term — ask your account manager.

<Warning>
`LastFeeCurency` on an execution report **may differ from the cumulative
`FeeCurency`** on the same order. An accumulator that sums `LastFee` across fills
into one running total is adding unlike currencies as soon as that happens, and
the result is a number that looks like a fee and is not one. Key per-fill
accumulation on the (fee, currency) pair, or take the cumulative figure from the
[`Trade`](/trading-api/websocket/streams/trade) record instead of building your
own.
</Warning>

## The Trade record is the economic record

`Trade` carries `Quantity`, `Amount`, `Fee`, `FeeCurency`, and `TradeStatus` —
the economics of the fill in one message, which the order streams do not provide.

<Note>
`TradeStatus` can move to `Canceled` after a trade has appeared as `Pending` or
`Confirmed`. A cost or P&L series keyed on the first message it saw will carry
fees for trades that were never settled. Key on `TradeID` and apply the latest
status — see [Trade lifecycle](/guides/trading/trade-lifecycle).
</Note>

Pricing is not a function of the channel, so choose one on workflow grounds — see
[Execution channels](/guides/trading/execution-channels).

## Network fees on crypto withdrawals

An on-chain withdrawal pays a network fee to the validators or miners of that
chain. It is not a Trillion Digital charge and it varies with congestion. Where
an asset is issued on more than one network, the network you withdraw over is the
largest fee variable you control — see
[Assets and networks](/guides/trading/assets-and-networks).

**How the network fee is applied to your transfer is a commercial term**: it may
be deducted from the amount you send or charged alongside it. See
[Crypto settlement](/guides/settlement/crypto#network-fees).

<Warning>
Establish which of those two applies **before** you instruct a payment that must
arrive at an exact figure. If the fee is deducted from the transfer and your
counterparty expects a precise amount — an invoice, a margin call, a loan
repayment — they receive short, and the shortfall has to be topped up with a
second on-chain transfer that pays a second network fee. Nothing about the
instruction will look wrong at the time you send it.
</Warning>

<Note>
Which assets and networks are enabled on your account depends on the Trillion
Digital [entity](/guides/accounts/entities) you face and your commercial terms.
Anything named in these guides is what the platform models, not a list of what is
available to you — your account manager confirms what is actually enabled.
</Note>

## What only your account manager can tell you

Ask for these together, once, rather than discovering them per trade:

- The basis on which your price is made, and how it varies with size.
- Whether a fee is charged inside the quoted price or alongside it.
- The currency fees are charged in, and whether that differs by instrument.
- How network fees on crypto withdrawals are applied — deducted or charged separately.
- Minimum and maximum sizes, and any credit terms that bear on them.
